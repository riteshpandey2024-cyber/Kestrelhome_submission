**Date:** 1 October 2026

**The decision.** Don't launch this as a "97% accuracy" flag. Launch it as a ranked list that tells the 40-a-month investigation desk which claims to look at first, and tighten the rule at a handful of outlets.

**Why not accuracy.** Only about 1 claim in 80 is fraud. A system that flags *nothing* was 98.7% accurate on past claims and stops no fraud. Next quarter we expect it to score about 96.6% - under the board's 97% - while our flagging list scores about the same. The number can't tell good from useless. Please give the board a rupee figure instead.

**The rupees (next 3 months, 120 investigations).**
- Expected fraud in the new claims: about 78 claims, roughly Rs 1.2 lakh.
- Checking our top 120: about 60 frauds caught, **about Rs 90,000 stopped - roughly Rs 750 per claim checked.**
- Cost: about 60 genuine customers held, ~Rs 23,000 of goodwill. **Net about Rs 560 per claim checked.**
- Honest range: Rs 400-1,100 per claim. Our June test caught 19 of 22 frauds, but that is a small sample. About half of flagged claims will be genuine, so staff should treat a flag as "look closer", never as "refuse".

**Your new-partner theory - right, with a catch.** New partners were clean until May. Since 1 May, claims under Rs 2,000 skip inspection, and fraud has more than doubled (1.1% to 2.5%). Nearly all of it is small, un-inspected claims from a few new outlets: **8 of the 45 new partners** involved, and **5 outlets account for 29 of 36 frauds.** Meenal is right too: most new partners are fine and should not be blocked.

**A warning.** A model built in April would have been *worse than guessing* in May-June, because the fraud changed shape. This must be re-run every month.
